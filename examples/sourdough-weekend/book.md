# Before you start

You need four things: flour, water, salt, and a kitchen scale. That is the whole shopping list. If you only buy one tool, buy the scale. Measuring bread in cups is how people decide they are bad at bread.

This guide runs Friday night to Sunday afternoon. The starter takes a week to build, so make it first and come back. Everything below assumes a starter that doubles within six hours of a feeding.

> Bread is not hard. It is slow, and the slowness hides your mistakes until Sunday.

Read the whole book once before you touch anything. It takes fifteen minutes.

# Friday night: mix

At eight in the evening, weigh 500 grams of flour into a large bowl. Add 350 grams of lukewarm water, which feels like nothing on your wrist. Stir until no dry flour remains. Cover it and walk away for an hour.

After the hour, add 10 grams of salt and 100 grams of active starter. Squeeze everything together with wet hands for about a minute. It will look wrong. A shaggy, sticky lump is correct.

## Why wait an hour first

Flour and water alone start to build structure without any kneading. Salt slows that down, so it goes in after. This is the only trick in this chapter, and it replaces ten minutes of kneading.

# Saturday: fold, then wait

Over the next three hours, stretch and fold the dough four times, about forty minutes apart. Wet a hand, grab one edge, lift it up until it resists, and fold it over the middle. Turn the bowl and repeat three more times. That is one fold.

By the fourth fold the dough should feel smoother and hold its shape a little. Leave it covered on the counter.

Your dough is ready to shape when it has grown by about half and has a few bubbles on the surface. Do not wait for it to double. Over-proofed dough is the most common reason a loaf comes out flat.

**Ask yourself:** can I see bubbles at the edge of the bowl? If yes, go on.

# Saturday night: shape and chill

Tip the dough onto a lightly floured counter. Fold the edges to the center, flip it seam side down, and drag it toward you in a short arc until it tightens into a round. Place it seam side up in a floured bowl lined with a towel.

Cover it and put it in the fridge for twelve to sixteen hours. The cold slows the yeast and lets the flavor develop. Most of the work is now done by time, not by you.

- Flour the towel more than feels sensible. Wet dough glues itself to cloth.
- If your fridge is crowded, a large bowl with a plate on top works.

# Sunday: bake

Put a heavy lidded pot in the oven and heat it to 245 degrees Celsius for forty-five minutes. Tip the cold dough onto a sheet of baking paper, slash the top once with a razor or a sharp knife, and lower it into the pot with the paper.

Bake with the lid on for twenty minutes. Remove the lid and bake for another twenty-five. The crust should be deep brown, darker than looks safe.

Then comes the hard part. Let the loaf cool for a full hour before you cut it. The inside is still cooking, and a hot cut loaf turns gummy.
